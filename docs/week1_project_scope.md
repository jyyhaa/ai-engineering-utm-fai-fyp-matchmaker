# Week 1 Project Scope Document

| Category / Field | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Project Name** | **UTM FAI FYP Matchmaker** (AI-Powered FYP & Supervisor Matching Assistant) |
| **Domain** | Higher Education / Academic Guidance / Explainable AI Recommendation Systems |
| **Problem Statement** | Undergraduate Artificial Intelligence students at Universiti Teknologi Malaysia (UTM) need to identify potential Final Year Project (FYP) supervisors whose research expertise aligns with their proposed project topics. However, lecturer research information and official FYP guidelines are available through separate university platforms, including UTM Scholar, FAI departmental webpages, and the FAI FYP Portal. Students may need to manually search and compare multiple sources to identify relevant lecturers and understand FYP requirements. This process can be time-consuming, particularly when a student's project description uses terminology different from the research expertise listed in lecturer profiles. Therefore, the proposed UTM FAI FYP Matchmaker aims to develop an explainable, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)-based conversational assistant that recommends potential supervisors, retrieves official FYP information, and provides evidence-based project scope suggestions with verifiable source citations. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1: Information is distributed across separate university platforms.** Lecturer research information is published through UTM Scholar and FAI departmental webpages, while FYP procedures and guidelines are provided through the FAI FYP Portal. Students must consult different sources to gather information for supervisor exploration and FYP preparation.<br>**Source/Citation:** [UTM Scholar – FAI](https://utmscholar.utm.my/faculties/fai), [UTM FAI Department of Intelligence and Informatics](https://fai.utm.my/department-of-intelligence-informatics-2/), and [UTM FAI FYP Portal](https://sites.google.com/utm.my/fai-fyp/).<br><br>**Evidence 2: Lecturer expertise requires interpretation and comparison.** Official FAI departmental webpages provide lecturer research expertise in specific areas, such as machine learning, natural language processing, and information retrieval. Students proposing interdisciplinary or differently worded AI projects may need to compare their project descriptions with several research areas to identify potential matches. This creates an opportunity for semantic retrieval and explainable recommendation support.<br>**Source/Citation:** [UTM FAI Department of Intelligence and Informatics](https://fai.utm.my/department-of-intelligence-informatics-2/) and [UTM Scholar – FAI](https://utmscholar.utm.my/faculties/fai). |
| **Initial Related Work (at least 3)** | **Study / Solution 1: Official Faculty Directories and Research Profiles.**<br>**Source:** UTM Scholar and UTM FAI departmental webpages.<br>**Key Limitation:** These sources provide lecturer information and research expertise, but do not directly evaluate the semantic relevance of a student's proposed FYP abstract against multiple potential supervisors or generate a consolidated explanation of supervisor suitability.<br><br>**Study / Solution 2: General-Purpose Conversational AI Assistants (e.g., ChatGPT and Claude).**<br>**Source:** General-purpose LLM-based conversational applications.<br>**Key Limitation:** Without retrieving verified and up-to-date UTM FAI information, generated recommendations may contain unsupported lecturer expertise, outdated information, or incorrect FYP procedures. General-purpose AI responses therefore require additional source verification.<br><br>**Study / Solution 3: Official FYP Portals and Handbook Documents.**<br>**Source:** UTM FAI FYP Portal and official FYP handbook.<br>**Key Limitation:** These sources provide important academic procedures and requirements, but students must locate, read, and interpret the relevant sections themselves. They do not directly provide conversational supervisor matching or personalized explanations based on a student's project idea. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | Existing university information sources provide lecturer expertise and FYP requirements but do not offer an integrated, document-grounded conversational system that semantically matches student project proposals with potential supervisors while explaining recommendations using verifiable evidence. A RAG-based approach can combine retrieval from official university documents with language-model-generated explanations to support more efficient and transparent FYP preparation. |
| **Initial Project Objectives** | **O1:** To design and implement a document-grounded retrieval pipeline using verified UTM FAI lecturer research expertise and official FYP guidelines.<br><br>**O2:** To develop an explainable RAG-based conversational assistant that recommends potential FYP supervisors based on student project descriptions and provides evidence-based project scope refinement suggestions.<br><br>**O3:** To evaluate the system in terms of retrieval relevance, supervisor recommendation correctness, citation grounding, response latency, and responsible AI safeguards. |
| **Target Users** | Third- and fourth-year Bachelor in Artificial Intelligence with Honours students at UTM FAI who are preparing FYP proposals, exploring potential supervisors, or seeking guidance on official FYP requirements. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | **Source 1: UTM FAI Official FYP Portal and Handbook.**<br>**URL:** https://sites.google.com/utm.my/fai-fyp/<br>**Data Type:** PDF documents and official university webpages.<br>**Purpose:** Retrieve FYP procedures, submission requirements, guidelines, and evaluation information.<br><br>**Source 2: UTM FAI Departmental Lecturer Profiles.**<br>**URL:** https://fai.utm.my/department-of-intelligence-informatics-2/<br>**Data Type:** Official faculty webpages, with verified lecturer information prepared as TXT or JSON records for ingestion.<br>**Purpose:** Obtain lecturer names, research expertise, and source references for supervisor matching.<br><br>**Source 3: UTM Scholar – Faculty of Artificial Intelligence.**<br>**URL:** https://utmscholar.utm.my/faculties/fai<br>**Data Type:** Online research profiles and publication information, where accessible.<br>**Purpose:** Provide additional evidence of lecturer research interests and relevant publications to support recommendations. |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to retrieve information from university documents and generate conversational answers grounded in relevant source content. Both systems aim to help students access academic information more efficiently. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | Extends beyond general academic document question answering by analyzing student FYP project titles or abstracts, identifying potentially relevant lecturers through semantic matching, presenting explainable supervisor recommendations with source citations, and suggesting possible improvements to project scope. The system supports supervisor exploration rather than automatically assigning or approving supervisors. |
| **Responsible AI Risks** | **Risk 1: Hallucinated or Unsupported Supervisor Recommendations.** The language model may generate incorrect lecturer expertise, invent publications, or recommend lecturers without sufficient supporting evidence. This could mislead students during supervisor selection.<br>**Mitigation:** Retrieve information from verified university sources, preserve source URLs, validate lecturer identities, and require supporting citations for recommendations.<br><br>**Risk 2: Fairness and Data Representation Bias.** Lecturers with more publications or detailed online profiles may receive higher recommendation rankings, while qualified lecturers with limited publicly available information may be overlooked.<br>**Mitigation:** Use comparable lecturer-profile representations, evaluate recommendations across different AI research areas, and avoid treating publication quantity as the sole indicator of suitability.<br><br>**Risk 3: Over-reliance on AI-Generated Academic Advice.** Students may assume that recommended supervisors are available or that suggested project scopes have been officially approved by the faculty.<br>**Mitigation:** Clearly state that recommendations are advisory, distinguish verified information from generated suggestions, and remind students to confirm supervisor availability and FYP approval with the relevant academic staff or committee. |
