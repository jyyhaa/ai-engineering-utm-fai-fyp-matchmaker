# Week 1 Project Scope Document

| Category / Field | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Project Name** | **UTM FAI FYP Matchmaker** (AI-Powered FYP & Supervisor Matching Assistant) |
| **Domain** | Higher Education / Academic & Research Guidance / AI System Matching |
| **Problem Statement** | Undergraduate Artificial Intelligence students at UTM struggle to align their Final Year Project (FYP) topic proposals with suitable faculty research supervisors and past academic benchmarks. Information regarding staff research domains, publications, and FYP committee guidelines is scattered across separate portals, causing students to rely on unguided keyword searches or informal advice that fails to capture semantic research alignment. An AI engineering approach enables an explainable, document-grounded recommendation system that matches student project proposals to supervisor expertise and past FYP benchmarks with evidence-backed reasoning. |
| **Evidence of the Problem** | **Evidence 1:** Academic staff expertise, research lab affiliations, and publication profiles are hosted separately from the official FYP committee guidelines and assessment rubrics.<br>*Source/Citation:* UTM Scholar FAI Research Experts Archive (https://utmscholar.utm.my/faculties/fai) & UTM FAI FYP Portal (https://sites.google.com/utm.my/fai-fyp/).<br><br>**Evidence 2:** Students frequently submit FYP proposals that overlap significantly with past projects or fall outside available faculty supervisor expertise due to a lack of accessible, centralized semantic search tools.<br>*Source/Citation:* UTM FAI FYP Committee Portal & Guidelines (https://sites.google.com/utm.my/fai-fyp/). |
| **Initial Related Work (at least 3)** | **Study / Solution 1:** Static Directory Webpages & Staff Lists.<br>*Source:* UTM FAI Staff & Management Portal.<br>*Key Limitation:* Only supports exact keyword/name search; cannot evaluate semantic overlap between a student's project idea and a supervisor's research publications.<br><br>**Study / Solution 2:** General-Purpose Conversational AI (e.g., ChatGPT / Claude).<br>*Source:* Standard LLM Interfaces.<br>*Key Limitation:* Lacks grounding in official UTM FAI faculty datasets and tends to hallucinate non-existent professors, research labs, or course policies.<br><br>**Study / Solution 3:** Traditional FYP Document Archives.<br>*Source:* Faculty PDF Repositories & Manuals.<br>*Key Limitation:* Relies strictly on exact keyword matching and lacks explainable recommendation logic for refining project scopes. |
| **Engineering Gap / Limitation** | Existing approaches either rely on rigid keyword search without semantic understanding or use general AI models that lack grounding in official university faculty data and cannot provide evidence-backed supervisor recommendations. |
| **Initial Project Objectives** | **O1:** To design a document-grounded retrieval system using official UTM FAI faculty research profiles and FYP portal guidelines.<br>**O2:** To develop an explainable recommendation pipeline that matches student project proposals with supervisor profiles, related past projects, and scope refinement suggestions.<br>**O3:** To evaluate the system in terms of retrieval relevance, recommendation faithfulness, groundedness, latency, and safety guardrails. |
| **Target Users** | 3rd and 4th-year Undergraduate Artificial Intelligence students at UTM preparing FYP project proposals and seeking supervisor assignment. |
| **Data Sources (at least 2)** | **Source 1:** *UTM FAI Official FYP Portal & Committee Guidelines*<br>URL: https://sites.google.com/utm.my/fai-fyp/<br><br>**Source 2:** *UTM Scholar FAI Research Experts Archive*<br>URL: https://utmscholar.utm.my/faculties/fai<br><br>**Source 3:** *UTM Faculty of Artificial Intelligence Staff Directory*<br>URL: https://fai.utm.my/management/ |
| **Similar to EduAdvisor** | Uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) over official university PDF/web documents to provide factual, grounded answers to student queries. |
| **Different from EduAdvisor** | Moves beyond simple Q&A by performing multi-component semantic matching: analyzing unstructured student ideas, generating ranked supervisor recommendations with citation evidence, and suggesting project scope improvements. |
| **Responsible AI Risks** | **Risk 1 (Hallucinated Matches):** Model invents research expertise or attributes non-existent papers to a professor, leading students to select inappropriate supervisors.<br><br>**Risk 2 (Fairness & Representation Bias):** Newer faculty members or niche research areas may be under-represented in the vector database, leading to recommendation bias toward heavily published senior professors.<br><br>**Risk 3 (Scope Safety Over-reliance):** Students might blindly rely on AI scope suggestions without consulting academic advisors, leading to unapproved proposal submissions. |
